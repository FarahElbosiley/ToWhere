# Lisbon Travel Notes

## Getting around
Lisbon is hilly. The historic trams (especially Tram 28) cover the steepest
parts of Alfama and Graça, but they're often packed with tourists — walking
or using the funiculars (Elevador da Bica, Elevador da Glória) is usually
faster and less crowded.

## Neighborhoods
- **Alfama**: oldest district, narrow medieval streets, fado music venues.
  Best explored slowly, in the morning before tour groups arrive.
- **Belém**: riverside district with the Jerónimos Monastery and Belém
  Tower. Also home to Pastéis de Belém, the original custard tart bakery.
- **Bairro Alto**: quiet by day, Lisbon's main nightlife district after
  9pm. Narrow streets fill with people holding drinks from corner stores.
- **Chiado**: shopping and cafés, including the historic Café A Brasileira.

## Practical tips
- Currency: Euro. Cards are widely accepted, but small cafés may be
  cash-only.
- A Lisboa Card gives free public transit and discounted entry to many
  museums — worth it for a 2-3 day visit with heavy sightseeing.
- Tipping is not obligatory; rounding up or 5-10% for good service is
  appreciated but not expected.
- July and August are hot and crowded; late spring or early autumn are the
  most comfortable times to visit.

## Day trips
Sintra, about 40 minutes by train from Rossio station, has several
palaces (Pena Palace, Quinta da Regaleira) and is very popular — arrive
early (before 9:30am) or expect long lines.
