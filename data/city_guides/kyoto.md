# Kyoto Travel Notes

## Getting around
Kyoto's bus network covers most tourist sites but gets very crowded during
peak season. A one-day bus pass is cost-effective if visiting 3+ sites.
The subway is faster for east-west and north-south trunk routes.

## Neighborhoods and sights
- **Higashiyama district**: preserved streets leading to Kiyomizu-dera
  temple. Best visited early morning or after 5pm to avoid tour groups.
- **Arashiyama**: bamboo grove and Tenryu-ji temple, on the western edge
  of the city. Gets extremely crowded midday; early morning is best.
- **Fushimi Inari Taisha**: thousands of torii gates up the mountain.
  Most visitors only walk the first 30 minutes; the upper trail is much
  quieter.
- **Gion**: geisha district, best for an early evening walk rather than
  photographing geiko/maiko directly, which is now restricted in some
  private alleys due to overtourism.

## Practical tips
- Currency: Japanese Yen. Cash is still important outside major chains,
  though card and IC-card (Suica/Icoca) acceptance has grown a lot.
- Many temples close their gates around 16:30-17:00; check specific hours
  before planning a late-afternoon visit.
- Cherry blossom season (late March-early April) and autumn foliage
  (November) are the busiest and most expensive times to visit.

## Day trips
Nara, about 45 minutes by train, has free-roaming deer in Nara Park and
Todai-ji temple, home to a large bronze Buddha statue.
