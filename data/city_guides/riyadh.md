# Riyadh Travel Notes

## Getting around
Riyadh is a large, car-oriented city, and until recently there weren't
formal street addresses in most areas — mail traditionally goes to PO
boxes, so getting around has historically relied on knowing nearby
landmarks. The Riyadh Metro is expanding and now connects to the airport,
but ride-hailing apps (Uber, Careem) and taxis remain the most convenient
options for visitors, especially given the city's size and traffic.
Renting a car is common for travelers planning day trips outside the
city.

## Neighborhoods
- **Olaya**: the modern business and dining core, home to Riyadh's most
  recognizable skyscrapers — Kingdom Centre and Faisaliah Tower — plus
  many of the city's better hotels. Good base for first-time visitors.
- **Diplomatic Quarter (DQ)**: quiet, green, and walkable, originally
  built for embassies. A calmer contrast to the busier central districts,
  good for a relaxed evening walk.
- **North Riyadh** (Hittin, Al Malqa, Al Yasmin): newer development with
  a strong café and casual dining scene, popular with residents rather
  than a classic "sightseeing" district.
- **Diriyah**: on the city's edge, this is Riyadh's heritage anchor — the
  historic mudbrick capital of the first Saudi state. At-Turaif, its
  restored core, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Practical tips
- Currency: Saudi Riyal (SAR).
- The weekend is Friday-Saturday, not Saturday-Sunday — relevant for
  planning which days attractions or offices are open.
- Climate is extreme in summer; October through April is the more
  comfortable and popular travel season, and accommodation can fill up
  quickly then, especially during Riyadh Season (an annual
  entertainment festival) — booking ahead is worth it during that
  window.
- Walking is best treated as neighborhood-specific rather than a way to
  get across the city; plan transport between districts rather than
  assuming you can walk it.

## Day trips
Beyond Diriyah (see above), a popular full-day trip is the "Edge of the
World" (Jebel Fihrayn), a dramatic escarpment about 1.5-2 hours outside
the city, usually visited with a guide or 4x4 tour given the unpaved
final stretch.
